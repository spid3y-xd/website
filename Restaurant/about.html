Heritage & Architecture

Quiet Luxury, Sculpted in Time

Born from the conviction that modern fine dining should abandon artificial ceremony in favor of profound ingredient purity, spatial serenity, and raw culinary craft.

The Genesis

An architectural canvas for modern gastronomy.

Founded in 2021 by Chef Marcus Vance and interior architect Simon Chen, Urban Fork was conceptualized as a quiet counterpoint to the hurried urban rhythm. Taking inspiration from Japanese minimalism and Northern European brutalism, the space employs basalt flagstones, hand-troweled lime wash, and acoustic slatted timber.

Here, there are no white tablecloths or stifling formalities. The exhibition kitchen stands directly open to the room, where patrons witness the raw choreography of embers, iron, and precision plating under tailored warm pendant light.

“Every design gesture in our dining room was calculated to draw focus to the plate and the conversation.”
Executive Chef & Co-Founder

Marcus Vance

Culinary Leadership

Craftsmanship honed across continents.

Chef Marcus Vance began his formal apprenticeship in Lyon under classical French masters, followed by formative years working in the kaiseki tradition of Kyoto. This synthesis of strict French saucier technique and Japanese respect for unadorned seasonality defines the culinary language of Urban Fork.

Before opening Urban Fork, Marcus served as Chef de Cuisine at three three-star Michelin establishments across Paris, New York, and San Francisco. His approach rejects frivolous garnishes; instead, every element on the plate exists for temperature, textural contrast, and umami depth.

The Brigade

Orchestrated Precision at the Pass

Behind every service lies an unwavering brigade of twenty-four chefs, sommeliers, and bakers moving with synchronized focus.

“Precision is our baseline. Hospitality is our soul.”

✦

Micro-Seasonality

Rather than standard quarterly menus, our dishes evolve weekly reflecting micro-harvests from twelve certified biodynamic regional growers.

♨

Open-Hearth Embers

We employ centuries-old binchotan charcoal and seasoned applewood, imparting subtle wood smoke nuances impossible to replicate with modern induction.

↺

Circular Gastronomy

Nothing of value is discarded. Vegetable trimmings, shellfish carapaces, and stone-fruit kernels are transformed into house vinegars, garums, and koji ferments.

◈

Bespoke Craft

Every ceramic bowl and presentation vessel was custom thrown by local potter Yuka Mori in coarse iron-rich stoneware, pairing touch with taste.
